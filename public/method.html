FLAPPY/NEAT METHOD OPEN THE LAB ↗

00 · OVERVIEW

Fifty small neural networks learn to fly by being bred, not trained.

Nothing here uses gradients or a pre-trained model. Each bird is a genome that describes a tiny network. The population flies one course together, the birds that get furthest have more offspring, and the offspring differ from their parents in weights and sometimes in shape. That loop is NEAT, NeuroEvolution of Augmenting Topologies (Stanley and Miikkulainen, 2002), and the whole of it runs in your browser at whatever speed you choose.

01 · SENSES

Four numbers per frame

Each bird reads the vertical distance to the centre of the next gap, its own vertical speed, the horizontal distance until it is clear of the next pipe, and the vertical distance to the gap after that. All four are scaled to roughly −1 to 1. The lookahead matters once the course tightens, because a bird that only knows the next gap tends to exit it heading the wrong way.

The network has one output, passed through a sigmoid, and the bird flaps on any frame where that value is above 0.5. In the lab, the lime lines on the leading bird are exactly these sensors.

02 · GENOMES

Structure that grows

Every genome starts minimal, with the four inputs wired straight to the output and no hidden nodes. Mutation then nudges weights and biases on most offspring, adds a new connection to about one child in seven, and splits an existing connection with a new hidden node in about one in seventeen. Splitting disables the old link and adds two new ones, the first with weight 1 and the second with the old weight, so the child behaves almost exactly like its parent and the new node only matters once later mutations change it.

Each new connection gets an innovation number that the whole run shares, so the same change made in two different birds gets the same number. Crossover lines the parents' genes up by those numbers, which is what lets two differently shaped networks have a sensible child.

03 · SPECIES

Protecting new ideas

A freshly added node usually makes a bird slightly worse before it makes it better, so if every bird competed with every other one, structural innovation would be weeded out almost immediately. NEAT avoids that by grouping genomes into species by a compatibility distance, which counts the genes two genomes do not share and adds half the mean weight difference of the genes they do.

Offspring are shared between species in proportion to each species' mean fitness, so a big species cannot crowd out a small one just by being big. A species that has not improved its best score in 15 generations is retired, unless it is one of the two best. The distance threshold adjusts itself each generation to hold the species count near the target you pick in the lab.

04 · SELECTION

What counts as good

A bird's fitness is the seconds it survived, plus two for every pipe it cleared, plus up to one more depending on how close it was to the gap's centre when it crashed. The last term breaks ties between birds that die at the same pipe, which is most of them in the first few generations.

Within each species the top 30% become parents, 70% of children come from crossover and the rest from cloning a parent, and every child is then mutated. The single best genome of the generation is copied over unchanged, so the best score never regresses because of an unlucky mutation. That carried-over champion is the lime bird you follow in the lab.

A generation ends when every bird has crashed or the survivors clear 100 pipes. By default each generation flies a new course, which rewards a policy that generalizes. Setting COURSE to FIXED replays the same pipes every time, which makes the curve smoother but lets the population memorize it.

05 · COURSE

A curriculum built into the level

The first gap is always centred. After that, gaps shrink from 176 px to 122 px over the first 60 pipes, while the largest allowed jump between one gap and the next grows from 130 px to 270 px. Early generations therefore get a forgiving start that rewards any sensible policy, and later ones are pushed toward precise control. On six test seeds, a population of 50 clears all 100 pipes in between 2 and 16 generations.

Play mode uses the identical physics and course generator, so your score and the ghost's score are measured on the same terms.

06 · BUILD

Static files, no server

world.jsDeterministic physics and course generation, shared by both modes.

neat.jsGenomes, innovation numbers, crossover, speciation and a compiled feed-forward evaluator.

trainer.jsSensors, fitness and generation bookkeeping on top of the two.

The simulation advances in fixed 1/60 s steps and the renderer interpolates between the last two, so motion is smooth at any refresh rate and a seed always reproduces the same run.
